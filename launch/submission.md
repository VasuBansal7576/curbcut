Curb Cut is a Chrome extension for keyboard, screen reader and low-vision users. It fixes accessibility barriers as you use real websites. Press Tab on Mass.edu and the About menu stays closed. Turn the extension on and the same action reveals the links inside it. [Launch film](https://github.com/VasuBansal7576/curbcut/blob/main/launch/curbcut-launch.mp4) · [15-second teaser](https://github.com/VasuBansal7576/curbcut/blob/main/launch/curbcut-launch-15s.mp4)

The film shows Munich Airport’s unnamed carousel button becoming “Vorherige Folie,” an ExtremeTerrain link becoming readable, and Kane replaying a keyboard task before and after its repair. On an unfamiliar page, Meridiano, the optional Jev naming experiment identifies “Menú” and “Buscar.” [Meridiano evidence](https://github.com/VasuBansal7576/curbcut/tree/main/proof/jev/live-naming-demo)

On OpenSeaMap, a site with no existing fix, the keyboard skipped the View menu. One shortcut reported the barrier, Grok wrote a fix in 16 seconds, and Kane failed the keyboard task without it, passed it with a mouse, and passed it with the fix. The fix then shipped in the extension. [Report-to-fix evidence](https://github.com/VasuBansal7576/curbcut/tree/main/proof/report-to-fix)

The extension bundles 106 fixes, 62 of them proven by Kane, and exposes the evidence for each one. The previous 105-fix installed-extension audit recorded 98 primary passes and seven unconfirmed results. [Live audit](https://github.com/VasuBansal7576/curbcut/blob/main/proof/reliability/submission-extension-e2e.json) The popup can generate a report with the specific change a site owner can make. Once the native site passes its check, the extension can retire the patch.
